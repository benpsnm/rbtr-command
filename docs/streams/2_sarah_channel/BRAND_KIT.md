## Colour Palette

**Primary: Warm Clay**
Hex: #C4745A
A terracotta-adjacent tone — earthy, grounded, warm. Not corporate, not clinical, not "wellness brand blush." This is a colour that looks good in a car park in Morocco and on a living room wall in Yorkshire.

**Secondary: Sage Mist**
Hex: #8FA58A
A muted, slightly dusty green. Pairs with the clay without screaming "spa day." Works well as a background tone on Instagram carousels.

**Supporting: Linen**
Hex: #F5EFE0
Near-white but warm. Background colour for slides, story templates, text overlays. Never pure white — too cold.

**Supporting: Bark**
Hex: #3D2E25
Deep warm brown — almost charcoal but with warmth. Used for text, outlines, never for backgrounds. Strong contrast against linen and sage.

**Accent: Pale Sunlight**
Hex: #E8D89A
Used sparingly — for highlights, key stat boxes, occasional pop. The colour of early morning through tent canvas. Feels hopeful without being loud.

**Never use:** Cool greys, bright whites, navy, black. Nothing that reads as clinical, corporate, or cold.

---

## Font Pairings

**Heading Font: Playfair Display (Bold or Regular)**
A serif with warmth and character. Has literary quality without being old-fashioned. Works beautifully large, on both light and dark backgrounds. Feels like a person, not a brand.

**Body Font: DM Sans (Regular, Medium)**
Clean, legible, modern — but not tech-cold. Pairs well with Playfair. Easy to read on mobile. The right choice for caption body text and captions on carousels.

**Accent / Quote Font: Cormorant Garamond (Italic)**
Reserved for pull-quotes and one-line statements. Beautiful in italic at large sizes. Don't overuse — when you see it, it should feel like a pause.

**Never use:** Lobster, any script font, Impact, anything you'd see on a gym logo.

## Tone of Voice Rules

**Who Sarah is (on camera and in captions):**
She's a Northern mum in her mid-30s who knows a lot about Pilates and is honest about what she doesn't know yet. She's training to be a qualified instructor. She's funny in a wry, dry, self-aware way — not a class clown, not trying to be funny. Just is, sometimes.

She does not perform wellness. She lives it, imperfectly.

---

**The 7 Rules**

**1. Speak as you would to a friend on the phone.**
If you wouldn't say it out loud, don't write it. "Optimise your wellness journey" is never a sentence Sarah says to her friends. Bin it.

**2. Short sentences. Northern pace.**
No winding clauses. No building to the point in paragraph three. Get there in sentence one.

**3. Be specific rather than aspirational.**
Not "feel your best self." Yes "your lower back will ache less by Thursday." Specificity is credibility.

**4. Name the uncomfortable thing.**
Pelvic floor. Leaking. Postnatal body. Diastasis. Burnout. She names the things other wellness accounts avoid. That's exactly why people trust her.

**5. Humour is earned, not inserted.**
Don't announce the joke. Don't add "haha" to explain it. If it's funny, it's funny because it's true. The kid sitting on her head during filming is funny because it actually happened.

**6. Never write down to the audience.**
"Even you can do it." "Perfect for total beginners." "You don't have to be an expert." These phrases subtly condescend. Trust that the person reading is capable of making their own judgement.

**7. No toxic positivity.**
She does not say "you've got this." She does not say "just believe in yourself." She does not use the word "journey" unless it's describing an actual road trip.

**Caption 4 — Myth-busting**
"I'm not flexible enough for Pilates." This is the thing people say that breaks my heart a little. Pilates isn't for flexible people. That's it. That's the whole list.
