# Built Dad — Day 56

**Day 56 is: Saturday 14 June 2026.**

---

## What Day 56 Is Not

It's not a graduation. There's no certificate, no post, no announcement. Nobody knows you finished except the people who watched you do it. That's the right number.

It's not a destination either. The man you've been building for 56 days doesn't retire on Day 56 — he just stops needing a programme to stay in shape. Day 56 is evidence, not finish line.

---

## What Day 56 Is

Day 56 is the day you close the programme and begin the rest.

It's the day you stand in the same place you stood on Day 1 and take your honest measure. Not against a perfect version of yourself — against the man who started Day 1 and wondered if this was worth doing.

It's the day you say to yourself, privately: *I kept the commitment.*

That matters more than any outcome. Men who keep private commitments to themselves become men their families can rely on. That's the throughline. That's what you've been building.

---

## The Day 56 Ritual

This should take about two hours total, spread across the day. Do it at your own pace.

### 1. Morning (alone, before the house is awake)

Sit in whatever your best quiet spot is. Take out the Day 56 prompt from the daily list:

> *"You made it. Not because 56 days is impressive — because you showed up every single day to something private and true and difficult. Say what you need to say to yourself. Then close the programme. Then go lead your family."*

Write or record whatever comes. Take your time. This is the last prompt — there's no next one coming.

### 2. The Photos

Put your Day 1 photo and your Day 56 photo side by side. Look at them. You don't have to say anything about it. Just look at them. You'll know what you see.

### 3. The Letter

Write a short letter to Hudson and Benson. One page maximum. They won't read it for years — write it to the men they'll become, not the kids they are now. Tell them what Built Dad was. Tell them why you did it before the expedition. Tell them what you want them to know about you.

Put it somewhere. Tell Sarah where it is.

### 4. Say Something to Sarah

Not a speech. Just something real and specific. What you figured out. What she made possible by being in this with you. You'll know what it is.

### 5. Evening — Do Something with the Boys

No reflection, no significance. Just be their dad for a couple of hours. Whatever they want. Let that be the last data point of the programme.

### 6. Close It

Wherever you keep your journal, voice memos, or notes from the 56 days — mark it closed. A line, a note, whatever feels right. "Built Dad — April to June 2026. Done."

That's it. Programme closed.

---

## The Keep-Going Plan

Built Dad as a formal programme ends on Day 56. What continues:

**Daily**: The evening micro-ritual practice stays. Not all 14 in rotation — pick the 3 or 4 that did the most work and keep using them. The daily prompt habit becomes a weekly habit: one question per week, your own question, however you've been asking them.

**Weekly**: The Sarah check-in rhythm stays. Not the scripted questions — just the habit of one real conversation per week that isn't logistics or the boys.

**Monthly**: One intentional day per month that is structured around fatherhood. Not performance — presence. One day where you're tracking what you're modelling to the boys, deliberately.

**At expedition launch**: Pull the Day 56 photo. Look at it. Then go.

**When it's hard on the road**: The micro-rituals travel. Cold water is available almost everywhere. Walking and voice memos work in any timezone. The grandad prompt works anywhere. These tools are yours now — they don't expire when the programme does.

---

## What You're Leading Them Into

The expedition launches in July 2027. That's thirteen months after Day 56.

By the time the truck rolls, Built Dad should be so integrated that you don't think of it as a thing you did — it's just who you are. The man Hudson looks at in the truck. The man Benson grows up knowing. The man Sarah built this with.

Your grandad's job was to model a kind of man for you to absorb. You absorbed him. Now you're the template.

Hudson and Benson are absorbing you.

Make sure what they're absorbing is the man you built.
