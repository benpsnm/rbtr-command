# Built Dad — Evening Micro-Rituals

14 options. Rotate through them. Each takes no more than 5–10 minutes. The point isn't to add a task to your evening — it's to create one moment of intentional closure before the day ends.

Pick whichever one fits the texture of the day. Some days you need the cold. Some days you need the quiet. Some days you need the boys.

---

## 1 — Cold Water + One Sentence
Get in the cold (shower, bath, whatever you have). While you're in it, find one sentence that describes today. Not a summary — one true sentence. Say it out loud before you get out. Let the cold be the full stop.

---

## 2 — Walk Peanut + Voice Memo
Take Peanut out alone, after the boys are down. As you walk, record a voice memo — just talk. What happened today. How you showed up. What you want to do differently tomorrow. Don't edit. Don't replay it. Delete it or keep it, either is fine.

---

## 3 — Read to the Boys, Then Sit
Read to Hudson and/or Benson as normal. After they're asleep, sit next to one of them for two minutes. Don't check your phone. Just look at your kid. Let whatever comes up, come up.

---

## 4 — The Day's Prompt (Written)
Take the day's Built Dad prompt and write an actual response. Even one paragraph. Not on your phone — on paper if possible. Handwriting slows you down in a useful way. Put the pen down when you've said the true thing, not the polished thing.

---

## 5 — Outside + Stars
If it's not raining and the sky is clear: go outside alone for five minutes. Look up. Think about the expedition — where the truck will be, what the sky will look like somewhere remote, what version of you will be standing under it. Come back in.

---

## 6 — The Grandad Seat
Sit in the same chair, or the same spot, each time you use this one. Spend five minutes thinking about your grandad. Not trying to generate wisdom — just his face, his hands, his voice. What he'd say about today. Let it be simple.

---

## 7 — Phone-Free Hour (Starts Now)
Put the phone in another room. That's the ritual. For one hour before sleep, nothing incoming. Sit with Sarah, read, do nothing. The ritual is the absence of the scroll — what you do in the space is secondary.

---

## 8 — What Hudson Said
Before you sleep, recall one specific thing Hudson said or did today. Write it down. Even three words. Over 56 days this becomes something. You'll be glad you kept it.

---

## 9 — Benson Snapshot
Same but Benson. One observation. One true thing about who he is right now, at nearly three. He will be different in six months. You are paying attention.

---

## 10 — Physical Reset
Whatever your body needs tonight — stretch, ten minutes of mobility, a few press-ups, a walk around the block. Not training. Reset. Do it with intention: you're maintaining the machine that will lead this family overland. Treat it accordingly.

---

## 11 — The Hard Conversation Rehearsal
If there's something you need to say — to Sarah, to Hudson, to someone else — and you've been putting it off: tonight, alone, say it out loud to no one. Full sentences. Hear yourself say it. It's always less terrifying than the version in your head.

---

## 12 — One Line to Nate (Unsent)
Write a message to Nate — one line — about what you're figuring out. Don't send it. Just write it. It clarifies what you actually think, because you're writing to someone who sees through the performance.

---

## 13 — Expedition Visualisation
Lie down. Close your eyes. For five minutes, picture one specific moment in the expedition. Not a highlight — a real moment. You're tired, the boys need something, the terrain is difficult, and you handle it well. Rehearse what that looks like, in detail.

---

## 14 — Nothing
Sit in silence for five minutes. No music. No podcast. No Built Dad prompts. Just whatever the day left behind. Let it settle. This is harder than it sounds and it's worth doing at least once a week.
