# Built Dad — Weekly Milestones

At the end of each week, check in against these. Not a grade — a temperature reading. The question is never "did you do everything perfectly?" It's "what's actually shifted?"

---

## Week 1 — Foundations (Days 1–7)
*Theme: Who you actually are, right now.*

### What you should have done
- Answered the daily prompts without softening them
- Spent at least one unstructured 30 minutes with the boys, no phone, no agenda
- Had one honest moment with Sarah that wasn't about logistics

### What you should have learned
- The difference between who you are performing and who you actually are when nobody's watching
- Where the PSNM era left residue that you still carry

### What you should have faced
- The first thing you've been avoiding naming. You won't have solved it. But you should have named it.

### Talk to Sarah about
What she actually sees — not a check-in about Built Dad, just asking her: *"What do I look like as a dad this week? Honestly."* Listen without defending.

### Model to the boys
Showing up to something hard without complaint. Doesn't have to be big. Getting up early, doing the cold, not cutting corners on something they can see. Let them see the choice, not just the result.

---

## Week 2 — The Boys (Days 8–14)
*Theme: Hudson and Benson, specifically.*

### What you should have done
- Identified one specific thing each boy needs from you that you haven't been delivering
- Done one thing with Hudson that was purely for him — his choice, his pace
- Held Benson without needing to be doing something else at the same time

### What you should have learned
- What Hudson is paying attention to in you (you may have to ask him sideways — "what does Dad do that's cool?")
- What Benson's face does when he sees you come through the door. You should know this by now.

### What you should have faced
- Whether you parent reactively (responding to what they need) or intentionally (shaping who they're becoming). Which is dominant right now?

### Talk to Sarah about
One thing each of the boys needs that only the other parent sees. Not a parenting meeting. Just: *"What do you see in Hudson right now that I might be missing?"*

### Model to the boys
That you apologise when you get it wrong. Not to teach them the lesson — because it's the right thing to do. They will clock the difference.

---

## Week 3 — The Marriage (Days 15–21)
*Theme: Sarah sees everything.*

### What you should have done
- Had one conversation with Sarah that wasn't about the expedition, the boys, or logistics
- Said the thing you've been almost saying
- Noticed her — not as expedition partner, not as co-parent, as your wife

### What you should have learned
- What your marriage actually costs Sarah in the current season, and whether you've been paying attention to that
- What your best marriage looks like versus what you've been settling for

### What you should have faced
- Whether you've been using busyness — expedition prep, RBTR, Built Dad even — as a reason to be half-present with her

### Talk to Sarah about
The expedition as a marriage choice, not just an adventure. What does *she* need to feel like a partner in this, not just a participant?

### Model to the boys
That you treat their mum like she matters. Specifically: that they see you initiate kindness toward her, not just respond to it.

---

## Week 4 — The Body (Days 22–28)
*Theme: Physical readiness for expedition leadership.*

### What you should have done
- Maintained whatever physical practice you're running — not perfectly, but consistently
- Done at least one thing that was genuinely hard physically and didn't tap out early
- Noted where your body is carrying tension and done something about it (walk, cold, stretch, rest)

### What you should have learned
- Where your body is ahead of where you need it, and where the gap is
- Whether you use physical discipline as self-punishment or genuine care for the machine

### What you should have faced
- The honest state of your physical readiness for months in a truck across rough terrain with two small children

### Talk to Sarah about
What the physical demands of the expedition actually are — not to worry her, to share the reality with your partner. Does she know what you're training for, specifically?

### Model to the boys
That taking care of your body is normal, not special. Not a performance, not a topic — just what Dad does. Hudson especially is watching.

## Week 5 — The Legacy (Days 29–35)
*Theme: What you inherited, what you're passing forward.*

### What you should have done
- Spent real time thinking about your grandad — not as a concept, as a man
- Identified at least one thing you do because of him, consciously
- Written or recorded something about him for the boys, even rough

### What you should have learned
- What you're carrying from your upbringing that's load-bearing (good, keep it) versus weight you don't need to pass on
- The specific shape of your grandad's influence — not "he was good" but exactly how

### What you should have faced
- Whether you've honoured his memory by becoming the kind of man he modelled, or whether you've let that slip

### Talk to Sarah about
Tell her something about your grandad she doesn't know. Not a story she's heard. Something that shaped you specifically.

### Model to the boys
Connect them to the family they came from. Tell Hudson one story about his great-grandad this week. Make the lineage feel real to him.

## Week 6 — The Fear (Days 36–42)
*Theme: Say it out loud.*

### What you should have done
- Named at least three specific fears without softening them into "concerns"
- Sat with one of them long enough to feel it instead of managing it
- Not acted on the fear — just acknowledged it as real

### What you should have learned
- The difference between fears that make you better when you take them seriously, and fears that just drain you
- Whether your fear about the expedition is rooted in something real that needs addressing, or noise

### What you should have faced
- The fear underneath the fear. Week 6 is the hardest week. You're supposed to feel uncomfortable.

### Talk to Sarah about
One fear — real, honest — about what the expedition could cost your family. Not to resolve it. To share it, as partners.

### Model to the boys
That adults are allowed to be uncertain. You don't have to project invulnerability. Hudson is old enough to see that his dad takes hard things seriously without being destroyed by them.

---

## Week 7 — The Commitment (Days 43–49)
*Theme: Re-choosing everything.*

### What you should have done
- Articulated your commitments — to Sarah, to each boy, to the expedition — with precision
- Removed one thing from your life that was making it harder to be the man you're building
- Reached out to Nate, or at least been honest with yourself about what he's given you

### What you should have learned
- What "commitment" feels like versus "intention." They are not the same.
- Which of your commitments have been coasting on inertia versus actively re-chosen

### What you should have faced
- If there's anything about the expedition, or about yourself, that you still haven't committed to — what's the hesitation?

### Talk to Sarah about
Ask her to re-choose the expedition with you. Not a big formal moment — just: *"Are we still doing this together? Are you still in?"* Give her space to say yes properly.

### Model to the boys
Follow-through. Pick one thing you told them you'd do this week. Do it exactly as you said. Let the pattern speak.

## Week 8 — The Hand-Off (Days 50–56)
*Theme: Ready to lead.*

### What you should have done
- Completed all 56 days. Every single prompt, even the ones you wanted to skip.
- Had the Day 56 ritual (see PROGRAMME_END_DAY_56.md)
- Written or recorded something for the boys that they can keep

### What you should have learned
- Who you actually are as a father, right now, not aspirationally
- What you're genuinely carrying forward and what you've set down

### What you should have faced
- Everything Built Dad asked you to face. If there's still something you've avoided, name it now. There's no Week 9 coming.

### Talk to Sarah about
What Built Dad meant. Not a debrief — a genuine share. You don't have to tell her everything. But tell her something real about who you were on Day 1 and who you are on Day 56.

### Model to the boys
On Day 56, do something with them that is purely about them. No programme, no reflection, no purpose except being their dad. Let that be the final data point they collect this week.
