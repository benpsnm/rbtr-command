# RBTR · Arocs 6×6 · Risk Register
**Top 20 risks ranked by Likelihood × Impact**

> Score: Likelihood (1–5) × Impact (1–5) = Risk Score (max 25)
> Priority: 20–25 = Critical · 12–19 = High · 6–11 = Medium · 1–5 = Low

---

## Critical Risks (Score 20–25)

### Risk 1 — Goldschmitt suspension delayed (custom spec, German lead time)
**Likelihood:** 4 · **Impact:** 5 · **Score: 20**

The Goldschmitt suspension is the longest-lead bespoke component in Phase 2. A bespoke German engineering item with 10–14 week lead time ordered in Week 5. Any delay — spec revision, production backlog, shipping hold — pushes installation from Week 14 to Week 17 or later, cascading Phase 2 by 3–4 weeks and compressing Phase 3.

**Mitigation:**
- Order confirmed by Week 5 (not Week 6 or later)
- Get production slot confirmation in writing from Goldschmitt, not just an order acknowledgement
- Identify alternative suspension upfitter (Hendrickson or local air suspension fabricator) as a backup option at Week 4
- Build 2-week buffer into subframe design (mount points compatible with alternative)

---

### Risk 2 — Fogstar / Victron sponsorship not confirmed; cash flow crisis
**Likelihood:** 3 · **Impact:** 5 · **Score: 15** (elevated to Critical given £19,800 combined exposure)

If neither Fogstar nor Victron confirm by Week 10, Ben must self-fund £19,800 in electrical components at a point when Phase 2 spend is already running. This is the largest single cash-flow risk in the build and could pause the entire build if funds are not available.

**Mitigation:**
- Approach both in Week 1 — earliest possible
- Prepare cash reserve plan: Phase 2 requires electrical order commitment by Week 12; confirm cash availability before Week 8
- If either sponsor declines, replace immediately: alternative LFP suppliers (CATL, EVE cells via UK integrator) and alternative inverter-chargers (Schneider Electric, SMA)
- Consider phased approach: order Victron first (more likely to respond quickly), Fogstar second
- Sponsor backup: EcoFlow Delta Pro Ultra (Tier 1 already approached) provides partial redundancy if Fogstar fails

---

### Risk 3 — Subframe fabrication overruns or quality issues
**Likelihood:** 3 · **Impact:** 5 · **Score: 15**

Subframe is Gate 1 on the critical path. If the fabricator underestimates complexity, encounters steel supply issues, or produces welds that fail inspection, this delays every Phase 2 install. A 4-week slip here can cascade to a 6–8 week departure delay.

**Mitigation:**
- Engage fabricator by Week 3 (not Week 5) — confirm their experience with HGV subframes
- Weekly check-in during fabrication; no passive monitoring
- Independent weld inspection at Phase 2 midpoint (Week 14–15) before suspension mounts are loaded
- Pre-agree a penalty clause or priority clause with fabricator if timeline slips
- Have a backup fabricator identified (not engaged, but ready to quote) by Week 10

## High Risks (Score 12–19)

### Risk 4 — Workshop access withdrawn or becomes unavailable
**Likelihood:** 3 · **Impact:** 4 · **Score: 12**

If the workshop arrangement falls through (landlord issue, lease dispute, access revoked), the build halts entirely. The Arocs is too large for a domestic driveway.

**Mitigation:**
- Secure minimum 12-month workshop lease with option to extend — signed before Week 2
- Identify backup workshop (HGV dealership workshop? Friend's yard?) before committing to Phase 2 spend
- Workshop insurance to cover tools and in-progress build

---

### Risk 5 — Major engine fault discovered (OM471 terminal)
**Likelihood:** 2 · **Impact:** 5 · **Score: 10** (treat as High given departure impact)

The Jaltest diagnostics in Week 4 may reveal significant engine problems: injector failure, head gasket, crankshaft wear. A terminal engine issue could add £8,000–25,000+ and 6–12 weeks.

**Mitigation:**
- Commission full Jaltest diagnostic before any Phase 2 spend is committed
- If fault found, get three independent quotes before proceeding
- Evaluate: repair vs source a lower-mileage OM471 replacement (used engine route)
- Budget contingency: £5,000 engine reserve built into financial model before build starts

---

### Risk 6 — Key sponsor pulls out mid-build
**Likelihood:** 3 · **Impact:** 4 · **Score: 12**

A confirmed Tier 1 or Tier 2 sponsor withdraws their commitment after the build plan has been designed around their product (e.g. Goldschmitt confirms, then pulls out in Month 4).

**Mitigation:**
- All sponsor agreements: written contracts with clear deliverables on both sides, not verbal commitments
- No irreversible design decisions based solely on an unconfirmed sponsor (e.g. don't cut holes for a specific product until it's physically in hand)
- Maintain a shortlist of alternative suppliers for every critical part
- Tier 1 fallback budget: maintain £10,000 in accessible cash to absorb one Tier 1 dropout

---

### Risk 7 — Habitation box fabrication delays (WS Sherburns slot overrun)
**Likelihood:** 3 · **Impact:** 4 · **Score: 12**

WS Sherburns is a specialist fabricator. If their production slot slips — other client overrun, staff illness, material delay — the box can't go on at Week 21, pushing Phase 3 and all interior work back.

**Mitigation:**
- Book WS Sherburns by Week 11, ideally earlier
- Monthly check-in during their build phase
- Identify alternative composite/aluminium body fabricator by Week 14 as contingency
- Chesterfield Composites (already a Tier 1 sponsor) may have fabrication capability — explore this overlap

---

### Risk 8 — Expedition Meister slide-out delivery failure
**Likelihood:** 3 · **Impact:** 4 · **Score: 12**

The slide-out mechanism is the single most complex mechanical item in the interior and must be ordered by Week 25–27 with an 8–12 week German lead time. Late delivery compresses Phase 4 interior work significantly.

**Mitigation:**
- Order confirmed by Week 25 (not later)
- Get weekly production updates from Expedition Meister from Week 30 onwards
- Design interior so ply lining Phase 4 work can proceed in non-slide-out zones while waiting
- Contingency: fixed-width interior (no slide-out) is a fallback — this changes the living space but departure date is preserved

---

### Risk 9 — Cash runs short mid-build
**Likelihood:** 3 · **Impact:** 4 · **Score: 12**

60 weeks of build with £60,000–70,000+ in parts and labour, partially offset by sponsors. If sponsor conversion rate is lower than expected (e.g. only 30% of Tier 1 confirm), the self-fund requirement could exceed available capital.

**Mitigation:**
- Monthly financial review: actual vs budget vs sponsor confirmed value
- Red line: if cash reserve drops below £5,000 before Week 30, pause non-critical spend and redouble sponsor outreach
- PSNM revenue: Ben's income from PSNM business is the primary cash buffer — protect PSNM revenue during build period
- Phased build approach: if cash constrained, Phase 4 (interior) has most scope for phasing — habitation box goes on, family can camp in shell while interior is finished
- Crowdfunding / channel monetisation: if channel grows during build phase, YouTube / brand deal revenue supplements

---

### Risk 10 — MOT / IVA failure on modifications
**Likelihood:** 3 · **Impact:** 4 · **Score: 12**

The Arocs has significant modifications: suspension lift, subframe additions, habitation body, electrical. The DVSA may require IVA (Individual Vehicle Approval) for certain changes. An IVA failure or MOT rejection for a modification-related reason can take 4–8 weeks to resolve.

**Mitigation:**
- Engage DVSA/IVA specialist by Week 40 (not Week 55) — understand which modifications require formal approval
- All fabrication to be done by a registered fabricator with documentation
- Pre-MOT inspection by independent HGV engineer at Week 51
- Book MOT slot at a station experienced with modified HGVs (not a standard car MOT bay)

## Medium Risks (Score 6–11)

### Risk 11 — Michelin XZL 365/80 R20 out of stock
**Likelihood:** 3 · **Impact:** 3 · **Score: 9**

This is a low-volume HGV tyre size. Stock can be limited across the UK.

**Mitigation:** Check stock 12 weeks before needed (Week 31). Order by Week 37. Alternative: Michelin XZY3 or equivalent if XZL unavailable.

---

### Risk 12 — Ben illness / injury during build
**Likelihood:** 3 · **Impact:** 3 · **Score: 9**

Solo builder + family commitments. A 2–4 week illness during a critical phase (subframe, Phase 3 shell) delays the timeline significantly.

**Mitigation:** Build 2-week buffer into each phase. Maintain a list of trusted tradespeople (electrician, plumber, fabricator) who can step in for specific tasks. Health priority: this is a 60-week physical job — protect it.

---

### Risk 13 — Alu-Cab shipping from South Africa delays
**Likelihood:** 3 · **Impact:** 3 · **Score: 9**

South Africa to UK freight can take 10–12 weeks by sea. Port delays at either end are common.

**Mitigation:** Order by Week 37 (not Week 42). Alternative: UK-based roof rack fabricator (Front Runner, Flatline van gear) as fallback.

---

### Risk 14 — Family illness during Phase 6 (test/depart)
**Likelihood:** 2 · **Impact:** 4 · **Score: 8**

A serious family health issue in the final weeks (child hospitalisation, Sarah illness) makes departure impossible or unethical.

**Mitigation:** Cannot fully mitigate. The build includes a 4-day buffer. If illness strikes, 1 July soft deadline can slip to 5 July. Beyond that: discuss family-first policy explicitly with Sarah — departure date is a target, not a contract.

---

### Risk 15 — Electrical wiring fault found at road test
**Likelihood:** 2 · **Impact:** 4 · **Score: 8**

A fault in the 24V chassis or house system that doesn't surface until the truck is loaded and moving — loose termination, undersized cable run, ground fault in the body.

**Mitigation:** Use a qualified 24V auto-electrician for all high-current wiring. Full load test at Week 29 (Gate 3). Second full test at Week 50 Phase 5 review. No "I'll fix it later" on electrical.

---

### Risk 16 — Webasto or Truma installation causes CO leak risk
**Likelihood:** 2 · **Impact:** 5 · **Score: 10**

Diesel-fired heaters exhaust carbon monoxide. Incorrect installation — particularly the flue run — can allow CO ingress into the living space. For a family of four in a sealed habitation box, this is life-threatening.

**Mitigation:** Use a certified Webasto/Truma installer (not DIY). CO alarm installed (§22) before heater is used for overnight stays. Test flue with smoke detector in living space before first overnight use.

---

### Risk 17 — Slide-out mechanism failure after install
**Likelihood:** 2 · **Impact:** 3 · **Score: 6**

The slide-out mechanism could fail mechanically after installation — actuator failure, rail misalignment, seal leak.

**Mitigation:** Film the install fully (insurance documentation). Get warranty terms in writing from Expedition Meister. Test 50+ cycles before departure. Carry a spare Linak actuator on the expedition.

---

### Risk 18 — Regulatory change affects specific countries on the route
**Likelihood:** 2 · **Impact:** 3 · **Score: 6**

A country on the planned route introduces new vehicle import/carnet rules, bans foreign HGVs, or introduces new road tax that affects the build's legality in transit.

**Mitigation:** Route is flexible — no country is mandatory. Monitor overlanding communities (Horizons Unlimited, iOverlander) for regulatory changes from Week 40 onwards. Carnet covers most contingencies.

---

### Risk 19 — Content channel fails to grow; sponsorship leverage reduced
**Likelihood:** 2 · **Impact:** 3 · **Score: 6**

If the RBTR channel doesn't gain significant traction during the build phase, the sponsorship pitch (based partly on audience reach) becomes weaker for Tier 2/3 brands.

**Mitigation:** Channel growth is a separate workstream but is self-reinforcing with build content. Consistent publishing (1 video / 2 weeks minimum). Focus on quality, not frequency. Brands sponsor for authenticity + niche audience, not just subscriber count.

---

### Risk 20 — Customs carnet processing delay
**Likelihood:** 2 · **Impact:** 4 · **Score: 8**

If the carnet application is submitted late or has errors, processing takes longer than 6–8 weeks and the truck cannot legally transit certain countries.

**Mitigation:** Apply Week 51 (not Week 57). Use RAC or AA carnet service — they are experts in this. Double-check country list against current political/entry requirements before submission. Carry bond/guarantee as required by carnet authority.
