# Photography Shot List — 4 Woodhead Mews
**50 specific shots. Briefed for photographer hand-off.**
**Shoot day: target golden hour start (1hr before sunset) for exterior and fire pit. Wellness circuit best at dusk.**

---

## SECTION 1: HERO EXTERIOR (Shots 1–6)

### Shot 01 — PRIMARY HERO: Front Elevation Wide
- **What:** Full front of the property including mews setting, taken from the far end of the mews. Property centred, slight asymmetry preferred over dead-on symmetry.
- **Time of day:** Blue hour (20–30 mins after sunset). This is the shot. Warm interior lights glow through windows, exterior dark and composed.
- **Composition:** Portrait orientation for Airbnb listing primary image. Dark sky above, lit interior contrast below. The building should feel solid and deliberate.
- **Props:** Interior lights on, fire pit in background if visible, no cars visible in frame.
- **Do NOT include:** Bins, any temporary signage, adjacent properties if they are not in keeping.
- **Lens:** 24–35mm equivalent, shot from low (lens at 1.2m) to give building weight.

### Shot 02 — Front Elevation: Daytime Architectural
- **What:** Same composition as Shot 01 but daylight — overcast preferred (no harsh shadows). Shows architectural detail clearly.
- **Time of day:** Midday overcast or early afternoon.
- **Props:** None. Clean framing. No cars.
- **Do NOT include:** People, garden furniture out of place, wheelie bins.
- **Lens:** 24mm.

### Shot 03 — Mews Arrival Shot
- **What:** Perspective down the mews toward the front door, as if arriving on foot. The property at the end of the frame, doors visible, sense of arrival and privacy.
- **Time of day:** Golden hour — warm side-light along the mews surface.
- **Composition:** Landscape. The "entering a world" feel. Rule of thirds — property in far third, mews leading the eye.
- **Do NOT include:** Other residents' cars, recycling, anything domestic that breaks the atmosphere.

### Shot 04 — Front Door Close Detail
- **What:** Door hardware, handle, entry detail. The quality of the finish. Smart lock or door number if photogenic.
- **Time of day:** Any — even lighting preferred.
- **Composition:** Portrait, tight crop. Material quality is the subject.
- **Lens:** 50mm equivalent.

### Shot 05 — Property from Garden / Rear Elevation
- **What:** Back of the property showing garden/terrace, rear windows, wellness circuit context. Shows the private outdoor space.
- **Time of day:** Late afternoon golden hour, sun hitting the rear.
- **Props:** Fire pit with small flame if possible (needs lighter fluid on standby). Terrace furniture set naturally.
- **Do NOT include:** Anything unfinished if attic works affect rear elevation; ask host day-of.

### Shot 06 — Aerial-Style or Elevated Exterior
- **What:** If drone permitted, a pulled-back shot showing the mews context, the private garden, and the wellness circuit from above. If no drone: shoot from an elevated position (upstairs window looking down onto garden/hot tub).
- **Time of day:** Late afternoon.
- **Note:** Check local drone regs before shoot day. If not possible, skip and mark N/A.

## SECTION 2: LIVING SPACES (Shots 7–18)

### Shot 07 — Living Room: Wide Establishing
- **What:** Full living room, wide lens, showing sofa/seating, TV area, and ambient lighting working together. Dark, warm, layered.
- **Time of day:** Interior shoot — artificial light only. All lamps on, overhead off or dimmed.
- **Props:** Throw blankets arranged (not overly styled). Coffee or drinks on table but minimal.
- **Do NOT include:** Remote controls visible, phone charger cables, plugs in shot, any personal items.
- **Lens:** 16–24mm.

### Shot 08 — Living Room: Seating Detail
- **What:** Tighter shot of the sofa/seating area — texture, cushions, the quality of the textiles.
- **Composition:** Portrait. Pull of the materials. Warm lamp in background.
- **Props:** Single candle, small book or object, throw naturally draped.

### Shot 09 — Living Room: Fireplace or Focal Point
- **What:** Whatever the main focal point of the living room is — fireplace, artwork, architectural detail — tight and well-lit.
- **Time of day:** Interior.
- **Do NOT include:** Anything that doesn't serve the dark luxury aesthetic.

### Shot 10 — Dining Area: Set for Two
- **What:** Dining table set for two people — wine glasses, simple table styling, atmospheric lighting above.
- **Time of day:** Interior, all artificial light. Warm tones.
- **Props:** Two wine glasses with a small amount of wine (red). Simple centrepiece — single stem in a low vase or candle. No full place settings needed, just the intention of a meal.
- **Do NOT include:** Sauce bottles, placemats that look cheap, tablecloths unless they are part of the design.

### Shot 11 — Dining Area: Wide with Kitchen Through-Line
- **What:** Dining area with kitchen visible in the background — shows the open-plan flow.
- **Lens:** 24mm wide.

### Shot 12 — Hallway / Entrance Interior
- **What:** The feel of arriving inside. Hallway or entrance, coat hooks, art if present, the transition from outside to inside.
- **Composition:** Leading lines — hallway depth drawing eye into the house.
- **Time of day:** Interior, controlled light.

### Shot 13 — Staircase
- **What:** Looking up (or down) the staircase. Architectural quality, banister detail, light at the top.
- **Lens:** 24mm from low angle looking up if staircase quality justifies it.

## SECTION 3: KITCHEN (Shots 14–18)

### Shot 14 — Kitchen: Full Wide
- **What:** Full kitchen showing the island (if present), all appliances, the scale of the space.
- **Time of day:** Shoot with all under-cabinet lighting and any feature lighting on. Mix daylight through window with interior light if window faces good direction.
- **Props:** Bowl of fruit or a cutting board with a few ingredients. Coffee machine on with a cup ready.
- **Composition:** Landscape. Show the entire run of units from an angle — don't shoot straight-on.
- **Do NOT include:** Washing up, cleaning products, any visible clutter on worktops beyond props.

### Shot 15 — Kitchen Island Food Styling
- **What:** The island styled as if the host has left breakfast or a welcome spread. Dark bread, cheese, fruit, a cafetière, olive oil — something that says "this kitchen is for people who enjoy food."
- **Props:** Dark wooden board or slate serving board. 3–4 hero items arranged but not over-styled. Linen napkin.
- **Lens:** 50mm from slightly above, slight angle.
- **Do NOT include:** Plastic wrap, anything with a supermarket logo visible, cheap-looking items.

### Shot 16 — Coffee Machine / Detail Shot
- **What:** Coffee machine close, a cup being filled or ready. Steam if achievable.
- **Composition:** Tight, portrait. Quality of the machine is the subject.

### Shot 17 — Kitchen: Hob or Cooking Detail
- **What:** A pan on the hob, slight heat visible, or a knife on a board — the working kitchen in use.
- **Mood:** Warm, real, not too styled.

### Shot 18 — Kitchen: Window Light Shot
- **What:** If there is a window in the kitchen, use available daylight to get a warm, simple kitchen shot. Natural, the opposite of the polished shots above.
- **Time of day:** Morning, if possible — soft daylight.

## SECTION 6: WELLNESS CIRCUIT — PRIORITY (Shots 27–40)

*These sell the property. Spend the most time here. Shoot at dusk — the wellness circuit with ambient lighting, warm water, and a dark sky is the defining image of the listing.*

### Shot 27 — HOT TUB HERO: Wide at Dusk
- **What:** Full hot tub, bubbling, lit from within, shot at dusk with property visible and lit in background. The establishing shot that communicates the offer instantly.
- **Time of day:** 15–20 minutes after sunset. This is the tightest window — plan shoot timing around it.
- **Props:** Two wine glasses on the hot tub surround (with wine). Candles on surrounding surfaces.
- **Do NOT include:** Garden hose, pool chemicals, any maintenance items. Clear the entire surround before shooting.
- **Lens:** 24mm. Low angle — lens at hot tub surround height, shooting across the water.

### Shot 28 — Hot Tub: Steam Detail
- **What:** Shot across the water surface with steam visible. Best in cold weather or early morning. Shows heat, texture, atmosphere.
- **Time of day:** Early morning if shoot day is cold, or dusk when air temperature drops.
- **Lens:** 50mm, shallow depth of field.

### Shot 29 — Hot Tub: Occupancy Shot
- **What:** Two people in the hot tub (host or stand-ins), shot from behind or at an angle where faces are not the subject. The feeling of being there, not a portrait.
- **Props:** Wine glasses, relaxed posture.
- **Do NOT include:** Faces (unless model release obtained and host agrees). Keep anonymous.

### Shot 30 — Hot Tub: From Above
- **What:** Directly above the hot tub (standing on surround or elevated position), looking down into the water. Bubbles, light, texture.

### Shot 31 — SAUNA HERO: Interior Wide
- **What:** Interior of the sauna, warm wood, ladle and bucket visible, rocks on the heater, low amber light. Everything that communicates heat.
- **Time of day:** Interior — artificial light only. The sauna's own ambient light is the source.
- **Props:** Towel on bench, ladle resting on bucket. Nothing else.
- **Do NOT include:** Plastic, any synthetic materials, modern intrusions.
- **Lens:** 16–24mm.

### Shot 32 — Sauna: Door Detail
- **What:** Door slightly ajar, warm amber light spilling out into the exterior. Shot from outside. The invitation.
- **Time of day:** Dusk — so the warm light reads against the cooler exterior.

### Shot 33 — Sauna: Bench Close / Texture
- **What:** Close on the wooden bench, grain of the wood, towel edge, heat-warmed surfaces.
- **Lens:** 50mm.

### Shot 34 — ICE BATH HERO
- **What:** The ice bath, full frame, showing the scale and the setup. Ice visible in water if possible (bring a bag of ice for the shoot if needed). Shot to look challenging and serious.
- **Time of day:** Any — works in daylight. Show the contrast with the sauna behind it or visible in frame.
- **Props:** Ice in water. Towel draped over edge. Nothing else.
- **Do NOT include:** Anything that makes it look makeshift or unconsidered.

### Shot 35 — Ice Bath and Sauna in Same Frame
- **What:** Composition showing both structures — communicates the contrast therapy concept in one image. Either from outside looking across both, or through a doorway/window.

### Shot 36 — FIRE PIT HERO: Lit at Dusk
- **What:** Fire pit lit and burning, golden hour / dusk, seating around it with glasses or drinks on the armrests. The evening winding down.
- **Time of day:** Dusk. The fire should be the primary light source in the shot.
- **Props:** 2–4 chairs around fire, drinks on armrests, fire burning well before shooting (needs to be established, not just lit).
- **Do NOT include:** Fire lighters, BBQ tools in the foreground, anything plastic.
- **Lens:** 24–50mm. Shoot slightly low so fire fills middle of frame.

### Shot 37 — Fire Pit: Faces Lit by Fire
- **What:** Two people seated at fire, lit only by firelight from below, faces warm. Intimate, not posed.
- **Time of day:** Full dark — no ambient light. Fire-lit only.
- **Props:** Drinks. Coats/blankets if cold.

### Shot 38 — Fire Pit: Detail of Flames
- **What:** Close on the fire itself. Texture of the flame, the coals. Atmospheric texture shot.
- **Shutter:** 1/500th or faster to freeze flame.

### Shot 39 — OUTDOOR KITCHEN: Wide
- **What:** The outdoor kitchen showing cooking surface, counter space, and the fire pit/terrace in the background. Landscape.
- **Props:** A few items on the counter — herbs, a bottle, something being prepped. Show it as a working kitchen.
- **Time of day:** Late afternoon — golden hour hitting the kitchen surface.

### Shot 40 — Outdoor Kitchen: Food on the Grill
- **What:** Something cooking on the outdoor grill — smoke, sear marks, movement.
- **Props:** Actual food. Meat or vegetables. Real cooking in progress.

## SHOOT DAY NOTES

**Preferred shoot structure (single day):**
- 09:00–11:00 — All interior rooms (kitchen, living, bedrooms, bathrooms, attic)
- 11:00–13:00 — Outdoor kitchen, daytime garden, dog shots, terrace
- 13:00–14:00 — Break / review / re-shoot interiors if needed
- 16:30–19:30 — Golden hour exterior, fire pit prep, wellness circuit at dusk
- 19:30–20:30 — Full dark night shots (fire pit, hot tub hero, full circuit lit)

**Equipment to have on shoot day:**
- Wine glasses (own — do not use host's)
- Bag of ice (for ice bath shot)
- Fresh flowers or single stem
- Dark wooden/slate serving board
- Quality coffee (make it on the day)
- Lighter and fire lighter blocks for fire pit

**Props NOT to bring or use in frame:**
- Supermarket-branded items with labels showing
- Cheap candles
- Plastic outdoor furniture (swap or move anything plastic out of shot)
- Visible power cables or extension leads
- Any unfinished construction materials
