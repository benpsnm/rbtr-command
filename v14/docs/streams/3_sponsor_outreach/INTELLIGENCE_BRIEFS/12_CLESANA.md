## Why RBTR Specifically

1. **Content gold.** The toilet question is the most-asked question about overlanding. "How do you go to the toilet?" gets millions of views. Clesana's answer to that question — on a 45-month expedition — is their best marketing.
2. **Extreme environment credibility.** No water access across Central Asia, the Sahara, and remote Australia. Clesana working without water supply is exactly the product's promise.
3. **Environmental responsibility content.** Leaving sensitive environments (national parks, wilderness) without contamination is something overlanding audiences genuinely care about.
4. **Family use case.** Two young boys needing a reliable toilet system is more relatable than a solo adventurer. More emotional connection.
5. **Small company + big exposure.** Clesana can't buy the exposure RBTR offers. This is their best available marketing vehicle.

## Risk Flags

- **Film roll availability globally.** The 45-month expedition takes the truck to countries where replacement film rolls may not be available. Negotiate a supply arrangement for the expedition (shipped ahead, or a large bulk stock).
- **Small company risk.** If Clesana faces supply issues or business difficulties, Ben needs a backup toilet solution (Laveo Dry Flush or similar). Don't build the bathroom around this product without a contingency.
- **The content is edgy.** "Toilet content" for a family expedition is light-hearted but some sponsors prefer not to be adjacent to bathroom content. Clesana doesn't have this problem — it's their entire product.
