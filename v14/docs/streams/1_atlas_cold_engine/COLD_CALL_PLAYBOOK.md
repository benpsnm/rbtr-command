## 6 Cold-Call Openers

### Opener A — The Busy MD (direct, no wasted words)

> "Hi, is that [Name]? It's Ben Greenwood here from PSNM in Rotherham. Quick one — we've got 700 pallets of short-notice storage space at six quid a week, no contracts. Do you ever run short on space, or do you know who'd handle that decision?"

**Why it works:** Respects their time. Gives the full offer in under 10 seconds. Ends with a pivot if they're not the right person.

---

### Opener B — The Gatekeeper (warm, non-threatening)

> "Hi there, it's Ben from PSNM, we're a pallet storage facility in Rotherham. I'm trying to get to whoever deals with your warehousing or logistics — could you point me in the right direction?"

**If they ask what it's about:**
> "We've got local short-term storage space — just want to make sure they know we exist in case it's ever useful. Probably a two-minute conversation."

**Never say:** "I'm calling about a business opportunity." They'll cut you off.

---

I just wanted you to know we're here because half the time when you suddenly need overflow space, you're trying to find someone in a hurry. Does that situation ever come up for you?"

**Why it works:** Speaks their language. Acknowledges the reality of warehouse life. Positions PSNM as the 'back pocket' solution before the emergency happens.

## 25 Objection Handlers

### Category 1: We Don't Need Extra Space

**1. "We've got our own warehouse, we're fine."**
> "That's good. Most of our customers have their own space — we're the overflow when it fills up, or when a seasonal load comes in that doesn't fit your racking. Do you ever hit capacity, even briefly?"

**2. "We're contracted with [national operator]."**
> "Fair enough. Are they flexible on volumes, or do you have a minimum commitment you're paying whether you use it or not? We're rolling weekly — you only pay for what you've got in."

**3. "We've got space in our own yard."**
> "Good position to be in. A lot of people come to us when they've won new business and need to ramp up before their next lease review. If that situation ever changed, it's worth knowing we're here. Can I send you a one-pager just so it's on file?"

---

### Category 2: Price / Value

**4. "Six pounds a pallet a week sounds expensive."**
> "Compared to what? If you're doing your own warehouse on a long lease you're probably paying eight to twelve all-in per pallet once you split your rent. We're six flat, no extras, and you can walk away any week."

**5. "We can get cheaper down south / up north."**
> "You can, aye. But if your goods are moving through South Yorks or the Midlands, the transport costs to go further will eat that saving. We're sat right off the M18/M1 — it tends to make sense geographically."

**6. "We'd need to see what that costs fully loaded."**
> "Simple maths — number of pallets times six, times weeks. No VAT surprises, no admin fee, no minimum. If it'd help I can send you a worked example for your typical volume?"

**7. "We've budgeted for a different provider."**
> "I get that. When does that budget renew? If things change or you need to put a backup option in front of someone, we're worth having in the file. Can I send you our one-page info?"

---

### Category 3: Location / Access

**8. "Rotherham's a bit far for us."**
> "Where are you based? [Listen.] We're five minutes off the M18 junction — depending on where your loads are going, you might find it's actually closer to your routes than you think. What does your distribution pattern look like?"

**9. "We need a site closer to our customers."**
> "Understood. If you're delivering into South Yorks, Lincolnshire, North Midlands or across to Leeds, we're pretty central. Worth a look at the map sometimes — M18 is underrated for reach."

**10. "We need access at specific times and we're not sure about 24/7 claims."**
> "I run the site. 24/7 access means 24/7 — there's no call centre, no booking window. You ring me. I'm not promising what I can't deliver."

---

### Category 4: Trust / Risk

**11. "We don't know you — we use established operators."**
> "Fair. I'm not going to pretend we've got forty sites and a PR department. What I can tell you is you're dealing direct with the owner, your pallets aren't going to get lost in a system, and if something's wrong you've got my mobile. How did the 'established operator' handle the last time something went wrong?"

**12. "We'd need references."**
> "Of course. I can put you in touch with a couple of current customers directly. When would be a good time to follow that up?"

**13. "What's your security like?"**
> "CCTV, secure perimeter, access controlled. I can walk you round the site any time you want — give me a day's notice and come and see it."

**14. "What happens if something gets damaged?"**
> "We're insured, and we handle goods with care. But honestly, the best answer is come and look at the site so you can see how we operate. Takes twenty minutes."

**15. "We've had bad experiences with small operators before."**
> "Tell me about it — what went wrong? [Listen genuinely.] I can't speak for whoever let you down but I can tell you what we do differently. If it's something specific I can address it directly."

---

### Category 5: Timing / Not Right Now

**16. "We're not looking to change right now."**
> "That's fine. When do things typically get reviewed for you — is it a quarterly thing, or only when a contract comes up? I just want to make sure we're on your radar at the right time."

**17. "We're going through a restructure."**
> "Understood, I won't push. Should I call back in a month or two, or is there someone specific who'd be handling logistics decisions after the dust settles?"

**18. "We've just renewed our contract."**
> "No worries. When does it come up again? I'll make a note and get back in touch beforehand. [Note the date.] And if anything spills over before then, remember we're here."

**19. "This isn't really our priority right now."**
> "I get it. I'll keep it quick — can I just send you a one-pager so it's in your inbox for when things quiet down? Takes me two minutes to send, takes you ten seconds to file."

**20. "We're happy where we are."**
> "Good — that's actually the best position to have the conversation, because you're not under pressure. If you're ever curious what else is out there, have a look at what we offer. Might be nothing. Might be worth knowing."

**21. "You need to go through our procurement system."**
> "That's fine. Who do I contact there, and is there a reference number or form I need? Happy to go through the proper channels."

**22. "Send me an email and I'll pass it on."**
> "I will do. While I've got you — who's the best person to address it to? And is that inbox actually monitored, or is there a direct email that'd be better?"

**23. "We use a preferred supplier list."**
> "How does a company get on it? I'd like to get PSNM listed so you've got us as an option when you need us. Is that something you can help with, or should I speak to someone else?"

> "No problem — who is? Or if it's easier, when's a good time to catch them? I'll ring back specifically for them."

**25. "We don't store pallets, we do [something different]."**
> "Ah right, fair enough. Sorry for the mismatch. Do you know anyone in your network who does deal with storage? We're always looking to expand our contact base."

## The Qualification Ladder

Ask these in order. Stop when you've got enough to book a follow-up or know it's a dead end.

**Q1 — Do you currently use external storage?**
> "Do you currently use any external pallet storage, or is everything on your own site?"

*Yes → probe their current setup. No → probe the pain.*

**Q2 — What's your current situation / problem?**
> "And when you do overflow — whether it's a seasonal spike or a big delivery in — where does that go at the moment?"

*Listen for: frustration, cost, unreliable suppliers, no current plan. Any of these is a warm signal.*

**Q3 — How many pallets and for how long?**
> "Roughly speaking — how many pallets would you be looking at, and would it be short-term (a few weeks) or something that might run a few months?"

*This sizes the opportunity. Under 20 pallets is T3. 50+ is T1.*

**Q4 — What would make you switch or start using us?**
> "What would need to be different from what you've got now for it to be worth considering?"

*Listen for: price, location, flexibility, reliability. Match your USPs directly to what they say.*

**Q5 — What's the timeline / next step?**
> "Is this something you'd be looking at in the next few weeks, or is it more of a 'back pocket for later' type thing?"

*Next few weeks = hot lead. Later = warm, set a follow-up date. No urgency and no pain = dead end, update CRM and move on.*
